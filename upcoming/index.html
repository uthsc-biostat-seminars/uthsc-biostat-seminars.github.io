Whether two exposures interact in their effect on survival is a common question in observational epidemiology. The conventional product term in a proportional hazards model measures interaction on the noncollapsible hazard ratio scale and restricts its form for multilevel exposures. I develop a causal framework for interaction between two multilevel exposures on the restricted mean survival time scale, which is collapsible and interpretable in units of time. The target estimands are population-standardized restricted mean survival times for each joint exposure level and contrasts that measure departures from additivity of the single-exposure effects. These are estimated by an augmented inverse probability weighted estimator that combines a generalized propensity score, an outcome regression, and a correction for censoring. The estimator is doubly robust with respect to both the exposure and censoring models, and its influence function supports design-based inference under complex survey sampling. A simulation study shows that the estimator has small bias and close to nominal coverage when a single working model is misspecified. We illustrate the method with NHANES data on smoking, alcohol consumption, and all-cause mortality, in which smoking is associated with a graded loss of survival time but there is little evidence of interaction.
