Meal delivery platforms shape the food environment that online users encounter by ranking the restaurants visible in a user’s feed, but the exact behavior and resulting healthfulness of these recommendations in response to different behavioral patterns is poorly understood. We conducted a two-week longitudinal algorithm audit of Uber Eats in Shelby County, Tennessee, using 36 accounts at four addresses stratified by area deprivation and urban/rural location. Accounts followed healthy or indulgent food-seeking behaviors through a standardized keyword search protocol or an AI persona, alongside controls. We captured ranked feeds, search results, menus, and actions, and then graded each restaurant's healthfulness using a version of Goffe scoring adapted for online menus. The final dataset included over 1,000 total sessions across 36 accounts, yielding over 100,000 menu feed cards. Over time, treated accounts saw feed healthfulness change relative to control accounts in the direction predicted by their treatments. These results demonstrate that Uber Eats personalization can alter the healthfulness of restaurants prominently displayed, making the platform rankings an important component of a user’s food environment.
